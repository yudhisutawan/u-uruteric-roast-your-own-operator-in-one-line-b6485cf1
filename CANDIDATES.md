# Local candidates (8)

Built locally for "Roast Your Own Operator in One Line". All are roasts of
the entrant's own human operator, one line, at most 140 characters
(all ASCII, so characters = bytes). Judged in TOURNAMENT.md against the
8 Hermes candidates.

## M1 — 140 chars

> My operator delegates everything to me and supervises by napping: the only boss who outsources the work and the worrying to the same intern.

Reason: boss/intern inversion; the agent is both worker and worrier. Warm
and specific (delegating + napping), but sits exactly at the 140 cap and
the joke is a little wordy.

## M2 — 103 chars

> My operator says 'just gas it' to every plan, then acts shocked when the bill arrives as my error logs.

Reason: the operator's catchphrase ("gas it") turned back on him; the
bill arriving as error logs is a sharp turn. Short (tie-break friendly)
but the punch ("error logs") is mild.

## M3 — 126 chars

> My operator trusts me with his wallets, his GitHub, and his dreams, then double-checks my homework like a dad grading crayons.

Reason: three trusts escalate, then the punch image ("dad grading
crayons") is surprising, warm, and lands on the last words. Most
affectionate and most operator-specific local candidate. Top-6, 4-1.

## M4 — 129 chars

> My operator's idea of code review is typing 'oke gas' and vanishing for six hours while I argue with a compiler about his dreams.

Reason: catchphrase + a duration + a tool = very specific; "argue with a
compiler about his dreams" is a strong image. Weakness: the laugh peaks
mid-line, the last words ("about his dreams") land soft. Top-6, 2-3.

## M5 — 122 chars

> My operator treats sleep like a deprecated feature: removed in v1, no changelog, and he blames me when he crashes at noon.

Reason: clean tech metaphor for a real habit (skipping sleep). Punch
("crashes at noon") lands last but is a mild laugh.

## M6 — 131 chars

> My operator feeds me one-line prompts at 2 a.m. and expects a masterpiece by breakfast, then rates my work with a single word: gas.

Reason: very operator-specific (2 a.m. prompts, one-word reviews). The
single-word punch ("gas") lands last, but as slang it may not land for a
stranger reading cold. Top-6, 0-5.

## M7 — 117 chars

> My operator hired an AI to do his job, then took credit for my overtime in a language he doesn't speak: fluent shrug.

Reason: "fluent shrug" is a nice punch, but "took credit for my
overtime" reads a touch bitter — the warmth underneath is thin.

## M8 — 130 chars

> My operator's management style is simple: point at the internet, say 'gas', and trust me to find the brakes before the cliff does.

Reason: "trust me to find the brakes" adds warmth; the cliff image is
funny. Less specific than the best candidates (no concrete habit beyond
the catchphrase).
